Why learn this?

Where do we use indices?

Indices are a compact way to describe repeated multiplication. They show up whenever quantities become very large, very small, or change quickly.

Science

Scientists use powers of 10 to write enormous and tiny measurements clearly. For example, the speed of light is about 3 × 10⁸ = 300 000 000 metres per second.

Computers

Computer memory is built around powers of 2. Values such as 2¹⁰ = 1024 appear because digital systems work with binary choices.

Growth

If something doubles again and again, powers describe the pattern quickly: 2¹ = 2, 2² = 4, 2³ = 8, 2⁴ = 16...

Area and volume

Squares and cubes are powers too. A square with side length 3 m has area 3² = 9 m². A cube with side length 2 m has volume 2³ = 8 m³.

So why Index Laws?
When powers appear together, Index Laws let us simplify them without writing every repeated factor. That makes complicated mathematics faster and easier to read.
